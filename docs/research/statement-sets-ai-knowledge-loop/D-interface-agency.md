# Set D — Interface agency

## Notes

**D-08 exists because the paper explicitly disclaims it.** "The mission is not to build Wikimedia's own ChatGPT." That disclaimer does real work — it makes the proposal sound modest — but the distinction between "a category of interfaces that make the knowledge commons directly available to readers" and "Wikimedia's own ChatGPT" is thin enough that it should be tested rather than assumed. If D-08 and D-01 draw similar agreement, the disclaimer is not landing, and the mission is more contested than its framing suggests.

**D-11 is the statement most likely to be uncomfortable, and the most important in the set.** It states the reader's-eye view: if the answer is right and it came from Wikipedia, what exactly was lost? The paper's answer is the editor and donor pipeline — an institutional concern, not a reader-facing one. Whether the movement accepts that trade openly is the crux of the entire mission, and it has not to my knowledge been asked directly.

**D-05 and D-11 are near-opposites and both are included.** This is intentional. Voting patterns across the pair separate people who accept the paper's framing from those who reject it, and that separation is more informative than either statement alone.

**D-02, D-09, and D-10 are the three distinct objections to interface-building**, kept separate because they are genuinely different: mission fit, opportunity cost, and reputation. Bundling them, which the paper effectively does by not addressing any of them, would hide which one actually drives resistance.

**D-03 is a concession statement.** It gives participants who broadly disagree with the paper somewhere to agree, which keeps the set from reading as a loyalty test.
