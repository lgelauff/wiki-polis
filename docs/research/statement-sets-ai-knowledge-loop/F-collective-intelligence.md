## Notes

**F-14 is the statement the paper would most firmly reject, and it belongs here.** The entire collective-intelligence principle rests on the premise that the human process is the point, not a means to a well-maintained encyclopedia. F-14 denies that. Some editors and many outside observers hold it. Leaving it out would make the set an affirmation exercise, and it is the natural stance-opposite of F-01 — the pair should be a clean cluster discriminator.

**F-06 relocates blame, deliberately.** The paper attributes editor decline to external forces: traffic loss, AI intermediation, generational shift. The competing explanation — that editing Wikipedia is unwelcoming, that newcomers get reverted and leave — is well documented in the movement's own research and appears nowhere in the paper. F-06 states the paper's version so that disagreement registers the alternative.

**F-10 is uncomfortable and factual.** The paper observes that community response to viral attention "tends to be defensive: locking the article, reverting changes, rather than engaging or onboarding the people arriving." Voting on a description of one's own community's behaviour is harder than voting on policy, and the result is worth having.

**F-03 and F-05 are the on-wiki AI questions**, which the paper mostly sets aside as already covered by the draft AI Guidelines. They are included because they are the AI questions most editors actually have, and a conversation with editors that omits them will read as evasive.

**F-09 is the counterweight to F-08.** The paper says the cluster should be "driven by institutional actors" who "closely collaborate with, and support those volunteers who want to engage." That is a real position with real arguments — speed, accountability, capacity — and it is stated here without hedging so that participants who hold it can say so.
