Wikimedia's distinctive value lies in the human process that produces its content, rather than in the content itself.
AI tools should help editors reach judgments rather than make editorial judgments in their place.
Editors should be permitted to use AI tools to draft article text.
AI-generated content submitted without disclosure damages the reliability of Wikipedia.
Wikipedia should require editors to disclose when they have used AI tools to produce content.
The decline in new editors is driven mainly by falling reader traffic rather than by conditions inside the editing community.
Recruiting new editors should take priority over developing AI technology.
Volunteer editors should hold a formal role in deciding Wikimedia's AI strategy.
Wikimedia's AI response should be led by institutional staff rather than by volunteers.
Wikimedia communities respond to sudden influxes of readers by protecting articles rather than by recruiting contributors.
Wikimedia should design AI-related volunteer tasks that people find worth doing, rather than tasks that mainly involve repetitive review.
Automating editorial work with AI will narrow the range of viewpoints represented on Wikipedia.
Wikipedia's model of negotiated consensus among editors cannot be reproduced by an AI system.
Wikimedia should accept a smaller editing community if AI tools let it maintain the same quality of content.
