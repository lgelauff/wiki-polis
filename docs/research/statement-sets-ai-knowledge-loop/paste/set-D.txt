Wikimedia should build interfaces through which readers reach the commons directly, rather than relying on search engines and AI assistants.
Building reader-facing AI interfaces falls outside the mission of an encyclopedia project.
Delegating the reader-facing interface to search engines was a reasonable choice during the search era.
Wikimedia will lose its direct relationship with readers unless it operates an interface of its own.
Any AI interface Wikimedia builds should send readers to Wikipedia articles rather than answering in place of them.
An AI interface that answers readers' questions directly would reduce the number of people who go on to edit.
Wikimedia should support several competing AI interfaces rather than building one official product.
Wikimedia should build its own conversational AI assistant.
Building AI interfaces would divert resources from maintaining the encyclopedia itself.
Wikimedia's credibility with readers depends on it not being seen as a company that builds AI products.
Readers who receive accurate Wikipedia-derived answers from an AI assistant are being served well, even when they never visit Wikipedia.
Wikimedia should aim to be one of the main interfaces through which people reach knowledge by 2030.
Wikimedia should build AI interfaces jointly with public AI developers rather than on its own.
