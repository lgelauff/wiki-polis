## Notes

**E-05 is the paper's strongest technical assertion and deserves the scrutiny.** "A state-of-the-art public AI model is a prerequisite for the mission to be complete. Without it, commons-aligned interfaces have no commons-aligned model to route to." That is a hard dependency on the most expensive, least controllable element in the plan — one the paper immediately concedes Wikimedia cannot build alone. If participants reject E-05, the mission has a viable reduced form; if they accept it, the mission's feasibility rests on partners.

**E-06 and E-13 look similar and are not.** E-06 is the paper's actual position: contribute data and norms to a partner-built model, retain influence over its alignment. E-13 is the weaker, more common position: just use what exists, build nothing. Separating them tests whether support for "partner rather than build" is support for the paper's ambitious version or its modest one. Expect these to diverge, and the divergence to be the useful result.

**E-11 versus D-01 is the real resource question, split across two sets.** Inward-facing augmentation and outward-facing interfaces compete for the same engineers. E-11 asks it as a priority question inside the technical set; D-09 asks it as an opportunity-cost question inside the interface set. Reading them together should show whether resistance to interfaces is principled or budgetary.

**E-12 is the objection from the editing community, stated plainly.** The paper worries about "reduced editor activity due to AI-related shifts in knowledge production" but files it under hypothetical. Editors watching newcomers arrive with AI-drafted text may hold it more firmly than that. It pairs with F-14.

**E-01 is expected consensus and kept anyway.** Quality signalling is the least contested item in the entire white paper, and it functions here as the calibration anchor, the same role C-01 plays in Set C.
