# Set C — Reciprocity and the terms of reuse

Pillar 1 of the mission. The paper's three-part reciprocity formula — attribution, transparency, contribution back — is split here into separately voteable claims, since someone can plausibly support one and reject another.

## Notes

**C-01 is included despite an expected consensus result.** It fails the scope guidance on its own — near-universal agreement carries little clustering signal. It earns its place as an anchor: it is the reciprocity demand with the broadest support, so its agreement level calibrates the rest of the set. If C-06 or C-10 approaches C-01's level, that is a much stronger finding than either number in isolation.

**C-07 is the paper's own surprising priority, put to a vote.** The mission text says interface visibility matters more than money ("delegation is no longer survivable"), and the reciprocity section says financial return is "only a first step." That ordering is not obvious to a movement that has just built a revenue product, and it is worth knowing whether the movement shares it.

**C-09 is an objection the paper raises implicitly and never resolves.** It proposes that AI platforms share data on "what users ask for, what answers receive low ratings, where coverage is thin" so communities can "allocate editorial attention." That is a description of commercial signals steering volunteer effort. Editors may well see the risk more clearly than the authors did.

**C-11 versus C-12 is the deliberate tension in this set.** C-11 favours a universal published standard; C-12 says bilateral deals cannot change the ecosystem. Both point away from the Wikimedia Enterprise model as currently run, from opposite directions. Participants who agree with both are endorsing something the movement does not currently do.

**C-02 and C-05 are the enforceability objections**, kept separate because they bite differently: C-02 is about power, C-05 is about legal and technical mechanism. Someone can hold either alone.
