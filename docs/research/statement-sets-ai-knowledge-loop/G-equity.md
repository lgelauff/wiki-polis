## Notes

**G-09 is the provocative statement the sharpness decision was about.** The paper states it as an operating assumption — "the overall assumption should be that Global Majority solutions can still benefit the Global North, but not the other way around" — and offers no argument. It is a strong asymmetry claim with real design consequences, and it is stated here flatly, without the surrounding framing that softens it in the original. G-11 is its counterweight, also stated flatly.

**G-05 is the hardest statement in the whole extraction.** In September 2025 the administrators of Greenlandic Wikipedia closed the project, citing AI-generated content as harmful to the language. The paper reports this as an indicator. As a policy statement it asks whether shutting a project can be the right protective act — which cuts directly against the movement's growth instincts. It is included because the case is real and the movement will face it again.

**G-13 and G-14 reach past AI into verifiability policy.** The paper raises the 2010 oral citations attempt as evidence that "Wikimedia's knowledge infrastructures themselves embed assumptions in which non-Western knowledge may not fit." That is a claim about core policy, not about AI, and it is the deepest equity claim the paper makes. G-14 makes it actionable and will likely be the most divisive item in the set. If this is judged out of scope for an AI conversation, drop G-14 and keep G-13 — but note that dropping both removes the paper's own strongest equity point.

**G-01 through G-04 are factual and mostly consensual by design.** They establish shared ground before the contested items. A set that opened with G-09 would produce heat before it produced information.

**G-10 is where equity stops being rhetorical.** Most movement documents commit to Global Majority *participation*. Authority is a different commitment, and the difference is exactly what "Global Majority partners need to play a key role" leaves ambiguous.
