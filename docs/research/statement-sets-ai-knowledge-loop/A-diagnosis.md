## Notes

**A-02 and A-12 are the load-bearing counter-statements.** A-02 tests whether participants accept the paper's causal attribution or read the traffic decline as a longer-running search-industry trend. A-12 states the position that undermines the entire grievance — that this was the deal all along — which the paper concedes on p. 27 and then argues past. If A-12 draws high agreement, Sets B and C are conversations about changing the deal rather than enforcing it.

**A-05 inverts the paper's own ranking.** The paper is explicit that infrastructure strain is "the lesser risk — the one that is merely visible." Putting the inversion to a vote tests whether the movement shares that ordering, which is not obvious: the infrastructure cost is measurable and the epistemic risk is not.

**A-09 is a value claim in a diagnostic set, deliberately.** The paper takes an unusual position for a Wikimedia document — that demand for frictionless knowledge is "legitimate and universal" and that the failure is Wikipedia's, not the readers'. That is a contestable value judgment presented as an aside, and it silently underwrites the whole interface-agency argument in Set D.

**Deliberately excluded:** the specific traffic percentages (18.4% spider share, 8% human decline, 35% combined). These are factual reports, not positions — voting Agree or Disagree on a measured figure produces noise. Their interpretation is captured in A-01, A-03, and A-05. See the residue log.
