## Notes

**This is the sharpest set, and the one to run first after A.** Every posture the paper discusses appears here stated in its own best terms. B-02, B-04, and B-05 are the positions the paper rejects; none of them is written as a straw man. B-04 in particular — exclusive licensing — is the one the paper treats most harshly ("not a defense of the commons but the mechanism that made it monetizable," on Reddit and Stack Overflow), so it is written flatly, as a live option someone might reasonably hold.

**B-07 is the hinge.** Nearly every proposal in Sets C through H assumes Wikimedia can extract concessions from AI companies. The paper asserts this repeatedly ("one of the few actors with the scale and legitimacy to shape these new rules") and never argues for it. If B-07 splits the room, that is the most consequential result in the whole exercise, because it means the movement disagrees about whether the mission is achievable rather than about whether it is desirable.

**B-09 is where the Paradox of Open becomes a concrete trade-off.** The paper frames openness and sustainability as reconcilable through an active stance. B-09 forces the case where they are not. Expect this to correlate strongly with B-02 and B-05, and to be one of the better cluster discriminators in the set.

**B-12 states an objection the paper never addresses.** Political engagement is treated throughout as an unalloyed good ("movement leaders taking visible, independent positions"). The neutrality cost is real and is raised in community discussions; leaving it out would make the set one-sided. It recurs in H-10 in a narrower form.
