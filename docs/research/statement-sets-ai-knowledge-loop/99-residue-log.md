**Reason:** Agree/Disagree on a measured quantity produces noise, not opinion structure — participants who don't know the figure will Pass, and Pass suppresses exposure for everything else in the pool. Where these figures carry a contestable *interpretation*, that interpretation is a statement: A-01 (attribution of traffic loss), A-03 (machine traffic trajectory), A-05 (which risk is greater), A-06 (Stack Overflow parallel), B-11 (whether $8.3M is material), G-01 and G-04.

**Better use:** put these in the conversation's framing material, not the statement pool. Participants should vote on interpretation with the numbers in front of them.

## 2. Historical narrative — background, not position

- The three-loop history: search (2001–), social media (2006–), AI (2019–). pp. 9–24
- Google's PageRank elevating Wikipedia and suppressing clones; >$10M in discretionary gifts before Enterprise. p. 12
- Knowledge Graph (2012); YouTube using Wikipedia against disinformation (2018); Enterprise launch (2021). pp. 12–13
- Casemajor's "coopetition" framing. p. 12
- Facebook Newsfeed (2006) and the shift to algorithmic ranking (2009–11). p. 15
- The 2014 Flickr 100M photograph training dataset. p. 27
- The foundational loop: Wikipedia's dependence on journalism, scholarship, heritage collections. p. 10

**Reason:** Narrative scaffolding. The one live claim embedded in it — that a loop which emerged on its own can dissolve on its own, so the next one must be built deliberately (p. 13) — is carried by B-06 and D-04.

**One genuine loss:** the paper's observation that the foundational loop "was generative, but not equitable" (p. 10) is a sharp and unusual concession. It resisted concretisation into anything voteable that wasn't already covered by G-12. Flagged in case a later pass wants to rescue it.

## 4. Rhetorical framings not converted

- **"Dark commons"** (pp. 29, 69) — the paper's central image. Retained as substance in A-13, dropped as a term. Voting on a metaphor measures reaction to the metaphor.
- **"Algorithmic colonisation"** (Birhane, p. 42) — substance retained in G-08, G-09, G-12. The term itself would swamp the claim.
- **"Epistemic capture" / "a single epistemological model"** (pp. 30, 35) — the paper's most philosophically ambitious claim. I could not reduce it to something both concrete and faithful; the nearest survivors are A-10 and F-13, and neither carries the full argument. **This is the largest deliberate loss in the extraction.** If one claim deserves rescuing in a second pass, it is this one.
- **"Parasitic rather than symbiotic"** (p. 54), **"hollow out"** (p. 41), **"seismic shift"** (p. 22), **"doom spiral"** (cited source, p. 35) — evaluative register, stripped throughout.

## 5. Out of scope for a Polis conversation

- Acknowledgements, commissioning, and process description (pp. 1–2). Includes the note that this is the first of three documents, with a governance brief and a technical direction paper to follow — relevant context, not a position.
- The forward reference to those two future documents (p. 1) — nothing to vote on yet.
- Descriptions of named external initiatives (Apertus, OLMo, Pleias, Bielik, SEA-LION, Masakhane, Transkribus, LiftWing) except where H-09 makes partnership a position.
- The Public AI Network's three pillars (p. 43) — a definition of someone else's framework.
- The 2026 Wikimedia Futures Lab results on video and personalised knowledge graphs (p. 17). **Borderline.** This is live community demand pointing toward interface change, and it belongs in the D-set framing material even though it isn't a statement.

## 6. Compounds that resisted clean splitting

**"Attribution that makes the commons visible rather than a hidden substrate; transparency about how commons-derived knowledge is used and transformed; and contributions back that strengthen the shared resource"** (p. 40) — split cleanly into C-01, C-03, C-06. Recorded because it is the clearest example in the paper of three separately-holdable positions presented as one commitment, and it is the decomposition the plan was written to catch.

**"By 2030, Wikimedia works in coalition with public AI developers and commons-aligned partners to build and govern AI interfaces and infrastructures through which the knowledge commons are produced and reach readers"** (pp. 6, 56) — the mission statement itself. Contains at least five claims: the coalition model, the 2030 horizon, building interfaces, governing infrastructure, and the primary-interface outcome. Distributed across D-01, D-12, D-13, H-06, H-09.

**Voting on the mission statement as written would be uninterpretable.** A Disagree could mean rejection of any one of five things. This is the single best illustration in the exercise of why atomicity is a measurement property rather than a style preference — and worth showing to the paper's authors directly.
